The Frontline Hiring Checklist

Work through it in order. Each section that has unchecked boxes is a place you're losing candidates.

01 Before you post

  • Split the requirements into true must-haves and skills you can train. Cut everything else from the post.
  • Check pay against 5–10 local postings for the same role. If you're below market, fix that first. No post will make up for it.
  • Lead the job post with pay, schedule, location and start date, before the company story.
  • Pick who interviews and block time on their calendars for the next two weeks.
  • Know what an empty seat costs you per day (overtime, missed output, turned-down work). Try our vacancy cost calculator.

02 Sourcing

  • Post where your workers actually look: job boards, local Facebook groups, trade and CDL schools, workforce centers.
  • Make sure someone can apply from a phone in under 5 minutes, with no account and no resume required.
  • Run a referral bonus, paid out in stages (e.g. at 30 and 90 days), and remind your crew about it every month.
  • Reach back out to past applicants and runner-up candidates before paying to source new ones.
  • Text candidates. Frontline applicants answer texts much faster than email.

03 Screening & interviews

  • Contact every qualified applicant within 24 hours. The good ones have other options by day three.
  • Do a 10–15 minute phone screen with knockout questions: license, schedule, pay range, start date.
  • Interview within 3 business days of the screen.
  • Ask every candidate the same structured questions and score them the same way.
  • Where it fits the role, use a skills test or short working interview instead of guessing from a resume.
  • Decide within 24 hours of the final interview.

04 Offer to first day

  • Make a verbal offer the same day you decide, then send it in writing.
  • Put every contingency in the offer letter: drug screen, background check, physical, references.
  • Get screens and paperwork scheduled within 48 hours of acceptance, not "sometime before the start date."
  • Track every pre-hire step with an owner and a status, so nobody stalls waiting on a result no one requested.
  • Have the new hire complete Form I-9 Section 1 by day one, and complete Section 2 within 3 business days of the start date.
  • Check in with the new hire at least weekly between offer and start. This is when no-shows happen.

05 CDL drivers: DOT qualification

  • A completed and signed employment application (49 CFR 391.21).
  • Motor vehicle records from every state where they held a license in the past 3 years, pulled within 30 days of hire (391.23).
  • Safety performance history requested from DOT-regulated employers in the past 3 years (391.23).
  • A pre-employment drug test with a verified negative result before any safety-sensitive work (382.301).
  • A full pre-employment query in the FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse (382.701).
  • A valid medical examiner's certificate on file (391.41–391.45).
  • A road test certificate, or an accepted equivalent such as a valid CDL (391.31, 391.33).

This is a summary, not legal advice. Confirm your obligations against current FMCSA rules.

06 The first 30 days

  • Have tools, PPE, uniform, logins and a supervisor ready before they walk in on day one.
  • Pair each new hire with a peer buddy who isn't their boss.
  • Have the supervisor check in on day 1, day 7 and day 30, and ask what's harder than expected.
  • Track 90-day retention by role and by hiring manager, and write down why people leave.
